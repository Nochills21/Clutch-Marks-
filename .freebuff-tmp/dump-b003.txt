  1: Proof using double angle formulas
  2: Proof using polynomial long division
  3: Proof using factor theorem
EXP: Use the fundamental identity \(\sin^2 \theta \equiv 1 - \cos^2 \theta\) on the numerator M1 [1]. Rewrite the numerator as a difference of two squares: \(1 - \cos^2 \theta = (1 - \cos \theta)(1 + \cos \theta)\) M1 [1]. Substitute this back into the fraction to obtain \(\frac{(1 - \cos \theta)(1 + \cos \theta)}{1 - \cos \theta}\). Cancel the common factor \((1 - \cos \theta)\) to arrive directly at \(1 + \cos \theta\), completing the proof A1 [1].
