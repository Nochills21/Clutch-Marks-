### Question bank

#### Tier 1 — Foundation and rapid recall (8)

**Q1.** The SI unit of length is the

- A. Millimetre
- B. Kilometre
- C. Centimetre
- **D. Metre**

> **MS:** The metre is the SI base unit of length; centimetres and millimetres are subdivisions.

**Q2.** Which of these is a scalar quantity?

- A. Velocity
- **B. Speed**
- C. Force
- D. Acceleration

> **MS:** Speed has magnitude only, while velocity, force and acceleration all have direction.

**Q3.** The prefix kilo means

- **A. 10^3**
- B. 10^-3
- C. 10^6
- D. 10^2

> **MS:** Kilometre, kilogram and kilowatt all mean one thousand of the base unit.

**Q4.** 25 mm expressed in centimetres is

- A. 250 cm
- B. 0.25 cm
- **C. 2.5 cm**
- D. 25 cm

> **MS:** There are 10 mm in a centimetre, so divide by 10.

**Q5.** The instrument used to measure the internal diameter of a test tube is the

- A. Metre rule
- **B. Vernier calipers**
- C. Measuring cylinder
- D. Stopwatch

> **MS:** Vernier calipers have jaws that reach inside a narrow tube and read to 0.1 mm.

**Q6.** A quantity with magnitude only, with no direction, is called a

- A. Resultant
- B. Component
- C. Vector
- **D. Scalar**

> **MS:** Scalars such as mass, time and temperature are fully described by size alone.

**Q7.** The SI unit of force is the

- **A. Newton**
- B. Watt
- C. Pascal
- D. Joule

> **MS:** One newton is the force that gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s2.

**Q8.** A metre rule with millimetre markings can be read directly to the nearest

- A. 0.01 mm
- B. 1 cm
- **C. 1 mm**
- D. 0.1 mm

> **MS:** The smallest division in the scale sets the resolution of a direct reading.

#### Tier 2 — Structured analysis and calculation (8)

**Q9.** A micrometer screw gauge reads 4.5 mm on the main scale and 0.28 mm on the thimble. The reading is

- A. 0.478 mm
- **B. 4.78 mm**
- C. 4.28 mm
- D. 7.30 mm

> **MS:** The reading is main scale plus thimble: 4.5 + 0.28 = 4.78 mm. [A1] The common slip is subtracting the thimble value instead of adding it.

**Q10.** A vernier caliper has a main scale reading of 2.4 cm and the sixth vernier division coincides. The reading is

- A. 2.4 cm
- B. 2.64 cm
- C. 2.406 cm
- **D. 2.46 cm**

> **MS:** Each vernier division is 0.1 mm, so six divisions add 0.06 cm to give 2.46 cm. [A1]

**Q11.** A trolley travels 150 m in 25 s. Its average speed is

- A. 3750 m/s
- B. 25 m/s
- **C. 6.0 m/s**
- D. 0.17 m/s

> **MS:** Average speed = distance / time = 150 / 25 = 6.0 m/s. [A1] Keep the unit as m/s, not m.

**Q12.** A car travels at 72 km/h. In metres per second this is

- **A. 20 m/s**
- B. 72 m/s
- C. 259 m/s
- D. 7.2 m/s

> **MS:** Multiply by 1000 and divide by 3600: 72 km/h = 72 x 1000 / 3600 = 20 m/s. [M1][A1]

**Q13.** A cylinder has radius 2.0 cm and height 5.0 cm. Its volume is about

- A. 31 cm3
- B. 126 cm3
- **C. 63 cm3**
- D. 20 cm3

> **MS:** Volume = pi r2 h = 3.14 x 4.0 x 5.0 = 62.8 cm3, which rounds to 63 cm3. [M1][A1] Using the diameter gives 126 cm3, a common trap.

**Q14.** A volume of 20 cm3 is measured with an uncertainty of 0.1 cm3. The percentage uncertainty is

- A. 0.05%
- **B. 0.5%**
- C. 5%
- D. 2%

> **MS:** Percentage uncertainty = 0.1 / 20 x 100 = 0.5%. [M1][A1]

**Q15.** A rectangle measures 12.0 cm by 4.5 cm. Its area, to the correct number of significant figures, is

- A. 54.0 cm2
- B. 5.4 cm2
- C. 540 cm2
- **D. 54 cm2**

> **MS:** Area = 12.0 x 4.5 = 54 cm2, and the answer is limited to two significant figures by the 4.5 cm value. [M1][A1]

**Q16.** A pendulum takes 32.0 s for 20 complete swings. Its period is

- **A. 1.6 s**
- B. 0.63 s
- C. 32 s
- D. 640 s

> **MS:** Period = total time / number of swings = 32.0 / 20 = 1.6 s. [M1][A1] Timing many swings reduces the timing error per swing.

#### Tier 3 — Examiner traps and synoptic depth (8)

**Q17.** Describe how to measure the volume of an irregularly shaped stone using a displacement can and a measuring cylinder.

- A. Measure the stone with a rule and use l x w x h
- B. Place the stone in a measuring cylinder of water and read the new level without the original level
- C. Weigh the stone and divide by its mass
- **D. Fill the displacement can to the spout, lower the stone gently into it with a thread, and collect the displaced water in a measuring cylinder; the volume of water collected equals the volume of the stone**

> **MS:** The can must be filled exactly to the spout first, and the stone lowered gently so no water splashes out. [B1] Reading the cylinder before and after also works if the stone fits and is fully submerged. [B1] Examiner warning: reading only the final level gives water plus stone, not the stone alone.

**Q18.** Explain the difference between precision and accuracy in a measurement.

- A. They mean the same thing
- **B. Precision is how close repeated readings are to each other, and accuracy is how close a reading is to the true value**
- C. Precision is closeness to the true value
- D. Accuracy is the smallest scale division

> **MS:** A set of readings can be precise but inaccurate if there is a systematic error such as a zero error. [B1][B1] Examiner warning: candidates often use the words interchangeably, which earns nothing.

**Q19.** Explain why a reading taken from a measuring cylinder with the eye above the meniscus is incorrect.

- **A. The line of sight is not horizontal, so the meniscus appears at a different level: this is a parallax error, and the eye must be level with the meniscus**
- B. The water reflects light
- C. The cylinder scale is inaccurate
- D. The cylinder is not vertical

> **MS:** A parallax error is a reading error caused by viewing from the wrong angle, and it is avoided by aligning the eye with the meniscus. [B1][B1]

**Q20.** A micrometer screw gauge reads 0.03 mm when its jaws are closed. Explain what this means and how readings should be corrected.

- A. The zero error cancels out if readings are repeated
- B. The gauge is broken and must be replaced
- **C. There is a zero error, and every reading is too large by 0.03 mm, so the zero reading is subtracted from each measurement**
- D. Readings are too small by 0.03 mm

> **MS:** A zero error is a systematic error, so repeating readings does not remove it; only subtracting the zero reading does. [B1][B1] Examiner warning: answers that say repeat and average have missed the point.

**Q21.** A student measures a wire diameter as 0.50 mm with a rule marked in millimetres. Explain why this is unreliable and how to improve it.

- A. Use a longer rule
- B. Measure the length instead
- C. It is accurate because 0.50 is exact
- **D. A millimetre scale cannot resolve 0.50 mm, so use a micrometer screw gauge and take readings at several points along the wire, then average**

> **MS:** The resolution of the rule is too coarse, and wire is rarely perfectly uniform, so several positions are measured. [B1][B1]

**Q22.** The diameter of a sphere is measured as 2.0 cm with an uncertainty of 0.1 cm. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the volume.

- A. 0.1%
- B. 1%
- **C. About 15%, because the uncertainty in the diameter is 5% and it is cubed**
- D. 5%

> **MS:** Percentage uncertainty in the diameter is 0.1 / 2.0 x 100 = 5%. [M1] Because volume depends on the cube of the diameter, the percentage uncertainty is 3 x 5 = 15%. [M1][A1] Examiner warning: adding instead of multiplying by the power is the standard error.

**Q23.** Classify each of the following as a scalar or a vector and give the reason: mass, weight, distance, displacement.

- A. All four are scalars
- **B. Mass and distance are scalars because they have magnitude only; weight and displacement are vectors because they have direction as well as magnitude**
- C. Weight and mass are vectors and the other two scalars
- D. All four are vectors

> **MS:** Weight acts downwards and displacement has a direction from start to finish. [B1][B1][B1][B1]

**Q24.** Explain why a stopwatch started by hand gives a less reliable time for one swing of a pendulum than for twenty swings.

- **A. Human reaction time causes an error of about 0.2 s each time it is pressed, so timing twenty swings and dividing spreads that error over twenty swings**
- B. One swing is too fast to see
- C. The pendulum is not isochronous
- D. The stopwatch is inaccurate

> **MS:** Reaction time is a fixed random error per press, so the fractional error falls as the timed interval grows. [B1][B1] Examiner warning: the improvement comes from a longer timed interval, not from repeating the single swing.

#### Tier 1 — Foundation and rapid recall (8)

**Q1.** Speed is calculated as

- A. Distance x time
- **B. Distance / time**
- C. Time / distance
- D. Force / area

> **MS:** Speed is the distance covered per unit time, measured in m/s.

**Q2.** The unit of acceleration is

- A. kg m/s
- B. m/s
- **C. m/s2**
- D. N

> **MS:** Acceleration is the change in velocity per second, so the unit is metres per second squared.

**Q3.** Weight is calculated as

- **A. Mass x gravitational field strength**
- B. Mass x velocity
- C. Force x distance
- D. Mass / gravity

> **MS:** W = mg, with g about 9.8 N/kg on the Earth.

**Q4.** The gravitational field strength on the Earth is about

- A. 9.8 kg/N
- B. 1.6 N/kg
- C. 98 N/kg
- **D. 9.8 N/kg**

> **MS:** A 1 kg mass has a weight of about 9.8 N, so g is 9.8 N/kg.

**Q5.** Newton's first law says a body stays at rest or at constant velocity unless

- A. It has mass
- **B. A resultant force acts on it**
- C. It is moving
- D. Gravity acts

> **MS:** A resultant force is needed to change motion, which is why frictionless motion needs no force.

**Q6.** Which quantity is a scalar?

- A. Acceleration
- B. Velocity
- **C. Mass**
- D. Weight

> **MS:** Mass has magnitude only; the others carry direction.

**Q7.** The resultant of a 6 N force and a 4 N force acting in the same direction is

- **A. 10 N**
- B. 24 N
- C. 6 N
- D. 2 N

> **MS:** Forces in the same direction add, giving 10 N in that direction.

**Q8.** The momentum of a body is calculated as

- A. Mass x acceleration
- B. Force x time
- C. Mass / velocity
- **D. Mass x velocity**

> **MS:** p = mv, measured in kg m/s.

#### Tier 2 — Structured analysis and calculation (8)

**Q9.** A car accelerates from rest to 30 m/s in 6.0 s. Its acceleration is

- A. 180 m/s2
- B. 0.2 m/s2
- C. 36 m/s2
- **D. 5.0 m/s2**

> **MS:** a = (v - u) / t = (30 - 0) / 6.0 = 5.0 m/s2. [M1][A1]

**Q10.** A velocity-time graph rises from 0 to 20 m/s over 8.0 s. The distance travelled is

- A. 160 m
- **B. 80 m**
- C. 2.5 m
- D. 20 m

> **MS:** Distance is the area under the graph: 0.5 x 20 x 8.0 = 80 m. [M1][A1] Using the full rectangle gives 160 m, the usual error.

**Q11.** The weight of a 5.0 kg mass on the Earth is about

- A. 0.51 N
- B. 490 N
- **C. 49 N**
- D. 5.0 N

> **MS:** W = mg = 5.0 x 9.8 = 49 N. [M1][A1] The common trap is reporting the answer in kg.

**Q12.** A 1200 kg car travels at 15 m/s. Its momentum is

- **A. 18,000 kg m/s**
- B. 80 kg m/s
- C. 1800 kg m/s
- D. 18 kg m/s

> **MS:** p = mv = 1200 x 15 = 18,000 kg m/s. [M1][A1]

**Q13.** A car at 20 m/s stops in 4.0 s. The deceleration and the stopping distance are

- A. 80 m/s2 and 40 m
- **B. 5.0 m/s2 and 40 m**
- C. 5.0 m/s2 and 80 m
- D. 0.2 m/s2 and 40 m

> **MS:** a = 20 / 4.0 = 5.0 m/s2 and s = average speed x time = 10 x 4.0 = 40 m. [M1][M1][A1]

**Q14.** Two forces of 3 N and 4 N act at right angles. The resultant is

- A. 1 N
- B. 12 N
- **C. 5 N**
- D. 7 N

> **MS:** Using Pythagoras: the resultant is the square root of 9 + 16 = 5 N. [M1][A1] Adding them gives 7 N and ignores the directions.

**Q15.** A force of 20 N acts 0.50 m from a pivot at right angles. The moment is

- **A. 10 N m**
- B. 40 N m
- C. 0.025 N m
- D. 20 N m

> **MS:** Moment = force x perpendicular distance = 20 x 0.50 = 10 N m. [M1][A1]

**Q16.** A resultant force of 8.0 N acts on a 2.0 kg trolley. The acceleration is

- A. 16 m/s2
- B. 0.25 m/s2
- C. 10 m/s2
- **D. 4.0 m/s2**

> **MS:** a = F / m = 8.0 / 2.0 = 4.0 m/s2. [M1][A1]

#### Tier 3 — Examiner traps and synoptic depth (8)

- **B. As speed rises, air resistance increases until it equals the weight; the resultant force is then zero, so acceleration becomes zero and the object falls at constant velocity**
- C. Air resistance is constant
- D. The mass decreases

> **MS:** Both the equality of the forces and the zero resultant must appear. [B1][B1] Examiner warning: 'terminal velocity is when it stops accelerating' alone is worth little without the reason.

**Q18.** A 2.0 kg trolley has a forward force of 12 N and a friction force of 4 N. Calculate its acceleration and state the direction.

- B. 2.0 m/s2 backward

- D. 8.0 m/s2 forward

> **MS:** Resultant = 12 - 4 = 8 N, then a = F / m = 8 / 2.0 = 4.0 m/s2 forwards. [M1][M1][A1] Examiner warning: dividing the applied force by the mass without subtracting friction gives 6.0 m/s2.

> **MS:** 200 cm3 of water has a mass of 200 g and a weight of about 2.0 N, which is the upthrust. [M1][A1]

#### Tier 3 — Examiner traps and synoptic depth (8)

**Q17.** Explain, in terms of pressure, why a drawing pin has a sharp point and a broad flat head.

- A. The point is decorative
- **B. The sharp point has a very small area, so a small force gives a large pressure that pierces the surface; the broad head has a large area, so the same force gives a small pressure on the thumb**
- C. The head increases the force
- D. The point reduces the force

> **MS:** Pressure depends on force per unit area, not on the force alone. [B1][B1] Examiner warning: saying the pin 'concentrates the force' misses the area argument.

**Q18.** Describe how to measure the density of an irregular solid such as a stone.

- A. Measure its weight in water
- B. Use a rule for all three dimensions
- C. Weigh it and measure its length and width
- **D. Measure the mass on a balance; measure the volume by displacement in a measuring cylinder or displacement can; then divide mass by volume**

> **MS:** Mass from a balance, volume by displacement, then divide. [B1][B1][B1] State the units, g/cm3 or kg/m3, for the final mark.

**Q19.** Explain why the pressure in a liquid increases with depth.

- **A. The deeper the point, the greater the weight of liquid above it pushing down, and this weight acts on the same area, so the pressure is larger**
- B. The density increases with depth
- C. The container walls press inwards
- D. The liquid is colder at depth

> **MS:** The argument is the weight of the column above divided by its area, which gives p = h x density x g. [B1][B1]

**Q20.** Explain why a steel ship floats even though steel is denser than water.

- A. Water does not press on steel
- B. Steel is not denser than water
- **C. The hull is hollow, so the average density of the ship including the air inside is less than that of water, and it displaces its own weight of water**
- D. It is pushed up by the engine

> **MS:** Floating depends on the average density of the whole object and on displacing its own weight. [B1][B1] Examiner warning: answering 'because it is light' without the average-density argument scores nothing.

**Q21.** A hydraulic jack has an input piston of area 4.0 cm2 and an output piston of area 80 cm2. A force of 50 N is applied. Calculate the output force.

- A. 1000 N
- **B. 1000 N: pressure = 50 / 4.0 = 12.5 N/cm2, so output force = 12.5 x 80**
- C. 250 N
- D. 4000 N

> **MS:** Pressure = 50 / 4.0 = 12.5 N/cm2. [M1] Output force = 12.5 x 80 = 1000 N. [M1][A1] Examiner warning: using the ratio of areas the wrong way round gives 2.5 N.

**Q22.** Explain why atmospheric pressure is lower at the top of a mountain than at sea level.

- A. The air is denser at altitude
- B. The air is colder
- **C. There is less air above the mountain pressing down, so the weight of air per unit area is smaller and the pressure is lower**
- D. Gravity is stronger at altitude

> **MS:** Pressure is the weight of the column of air above per unit area. [B1][B1]

**Q23.** Explain why a dam wall is built thicker at the bottom than at the top.

- A. To hold more water
- B. Because the water is denser at depth
- C. To look stable
- **D. The pressure of the water increases with depth, so the force on each square metre is greater at the bottom and the wall must be stronger there**

> **MS:** The pressure argument must be linked to the force on the wall. [B1][B1]

**Q24.** A block floats with half its volume under water of density 1000 kg/m3. State the density of the block and explain.

- **A. About 500 kg/m3, because a floating body displaces its own weight and half the volume is submerged, so its average density is half that of water**
- B. 1000 kg/m3
- C. 250 kg/m3
- D. 2000 kg/m3

> **MS:** The fraction submerged equals the ratio of densities. [M1][A1] Examiner warning: a floating body's density is never greater than the liquid's.
