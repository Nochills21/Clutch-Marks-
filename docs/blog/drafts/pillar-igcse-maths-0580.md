## What is IGCSE Maths 0580?

Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 is the most widely taken international GCSE maths qualification. It's assessed by **two written papers** at the end of the course, and your grade (A\*–G, with C usually the "pass" benchmark schools set) depends entirely on how many marks you bank across those two papers — there's no coursework.

**The two routes:**

| | **Core (Papers 1 & 3)** | **Extended (Papers 2 & 4)** |
|---|---|---|
| Grades available | C to G | **A\*** to E |
| Paper 1 / 2 length | 1h 30m, 50 marks | 1h 30m, 50 marks |
| Paper 3 / 4 length | 2h, 80 marks | 2h 30m, 100 marks |
| Content depth | Syllabus "core" sections only | Core **+ supplement** sections |
| Who takes it | Students targeting grade C | Students targeting A\*/A/B — and every sixth-form route worth having |

**If your school hasn't told you otherwise, you're almost certainly Extended.** Universities and top sixth forms generally want a 7/8/A/A* — Core caps you at C. Everything in this guide covers Extended, noting Core overlaps where relevant.

A calculator is allowed in **both** papers (a change from older specs — check your exam year). Papers mix short 1-mark items with multi-step structured questions worth 8–10 marks.

## The 0580 syllabus at a glance (2025–2027)

The syllabus has **four content strands**. Below is every topic, what it actually tests, and its weight in the exams. Percentages are the approximate Extended-paper weightings — use them to prioritise revision:

| Strand | Weight | Topics |
|---|---|---|
| **Number** | ~15% | Integers, powers, roots; fractions/decimals/percentages; ratio & proportion; indices & standard form; sets; time & money |
| **Algebra** | ~30% | Expressions & expansion; equations & inequalities; sequences; functions; graphs (linear, quadratic, cubic, reciprocal, exponential) |
| **Geometry & Mensuration** | ~25% | Angle facts & polygons; congruence & similarity; circle theorems; area/volume; bearings & loci; transformations |
| **Trigonometry** | ~10% | Right-angled triangles; sine & cosine rules; area of a triangle; bearings problems |
| **Statistics & Probability** | ~20% | Averages & spread; cumulative frequency; scatter diagrams; probability tree & Venn problems |

*(Weights vary slightly by paper session — the exam board publishes exact assessment objectives: ~40% of marks for "technique", ~35% "apply in context", ~25% "solve unfamiliar problems".)*

### Topic-by-topic breakdown

**Number** ([revise Number topics](https://clutchmarks.study/subjects))
- **Arithmetic and place value** — operations, order of operations, rounding, estimation. Marks come from showing clear working.
- **Fractions, decimals, percentages** — conversions, percentage increase/decrease, reverse percentages (the classic 3-mark question), compound interest.
- **Ratio, rate and proportion** — sharing in a ratio, direct/inverse proportion, unit rates, speed–distance–time.
- **Indices and standard form** — the index laws, negative and fractional indices, writing/using standard form. Almost guaranteed 3–5 marks per paper.
- **Sets** — Venn diagrams, union/intersection/complement notation. Popular as a 4-mark Venn problem.

**Algebra** ([revise Algebra topics](https://clutchmarks.study/subjects))
- **Simplifying and expanding** — collecting terms, expanding double brackets, factorising (including quadratics).
- **Equations** — linear, simultaneous (elimination and substitution), quadratic (factorising and formula). Show every line: method marks survive arithmetic slips.
- **Inequalities** — solving, number-line representation, **shade-the-correct-region** graph questions.
- **Sequences and functions** — nth term (linear and quadratic), function notation, inverse and composite functions (Extended only).
- **Graphs** — drawing and interpreting; gradients and intercepts; using graphs to solve equations. plotting accuracy earns real marks.

**Geometry & Mensuration**
- **Angles and polygons** — angle facts, interior/exterior angles, polygon formulae `(n−2)×180°`.
- **Congruence and similarity** — proving congruence (SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS), similar shapes' scale factors (area/volume use k² and k³ — a favourite trap).
- **Circle theorems** — the 6 theorems: angles in same segment, angle at centre, cyclic quadrilateral, tangent–radius, alternate segment, chord bisector. Learn the *reasons* word-for-word; reasons earn the marks.
- **Area and volume** — prisms, cylinders, cones, spheres, sectors and arcs. Formulae are given in the paper, but knowing when to use each is what's tested.
- **Bearings and loci** — three-figure bearings, measuring/drawing accurately, locus of a point conditions.

**Transformations and Vectors**
- The four transformations (reflection, rotation, translation, enlargement — including negative/fractional scale factors at Extended).
- Vector arithmetic, position vectors, and **vector geometry proofs** (Extended-only, the hardest single topic for most students).

**Trigonometry**
- SOHCAHTOA, exact trig values, angles of elevation/depression.
- Sine rule, cosine rule, `½ab sin C` — combined with bearings into 6–8 mark problems.
- Graphs of sin/cos/tan and transformations of them (Extended).

**Statistics & Probability**
- **Data handling** — mean/median/mode/range, grouped-data estimates, histograms, cumulative frequency (quartiles, IQR, box plots).
- **Probability** — tree diagrams (with and without replacement), Venn diagrams, conditional probability. "Given that…" wording changes the denominator — the classic lost mark.

## How 0580 is graded — and what an A* actually takes

Your two papers are scaled to a common grade scale. As a rough guide, Extended **A\* has historically required ~85–90% overall** (it drifts a few marks per session — Cambridge publishes thresholds after each exam).

What that means in practice:

1. **You can drop ~10–15% and still get an A\*.** You don't need perfection — you need to eliminate *silly* losses. Most A\* candidates lose more marks to misreads and arithmetic slips than to hard content.
2. **Method marks (M marks) are banked even when the answer is wrong.** A correct method with a slip still earns most of the question. An answer with no working earns zero. **Write every step.**
3. **Accuracy marks (A marks) follow method marks** — and are only awarded if your method was valid.
4. **Command words matter:** *Show that…* means prove rigorously (no "it just is"); *Hence* means use the previous part; *Explain* means a sentence, not a calculation.

## The 90-day A* plan

*(This is the same plan our [Study Planner](https://clutchmarks.study/study-planner) generates personalised to your weak topics — free.)*

**Days 1–20 — Diagnose and rebuild foundations**
- Take one recent Paper 2 and one Paper 4 **untimed**, syllabus open. Mark harshly with the mark scheme. List every dropped mark by topic — this list is your revision syllabus, not the textbook's.
- Drill Number and Algebra basics: fractions→percentages, indices, expanding/factorising. These underpin ~40% of the paper.

**Days 21–50 — Topic-by-topic mastery**
- One topic per day: 15 minutes of notes, then 30–45 minutes of **past-paper questions on that exact topic** (not textbook questions — exam questions have a style you must absorb).
- For every wrong answer, write **why** in a "mistake log": misread? method unknown? arithmetic? Number facts? Patterns appear fast.
- Hardest Extended topics to hit in this block: vector geometry proofs, circle theorem reasons, functions (composite/inverse), conditional probability.

**Days 51–75 — Mixed practice, timed**
- Full Paper 2 in 90 minutes, twice a week. Full Paper 4 in 2h30m, once a week. Mark with the real mark scheme, update your mistake log.
- Target: finishing with 10 minutes to check. Checking = re-reading command words and re-adding arithmetic in the last question of each part.

**Days 76–90 — Threshold simulation**
- Alternate papers, all timed, all marked. Your mistake log should be shrinking to "careless" entries only.
- Re-do every question you ever got wrong (your log makes this fast). Re-attempting past mistakes is the single highest-yield revision activity in maths.

## Past papers: how to use them (and where)

Past papers are the highest-yield revision tool in 0580 — but most students use them wrong. The rules:

- **Do them by topic first, then full-paper.** Jumping straight into full papers before you know each topic = frustration and wasted papers.
- **Always mark with the official mark scheme** — not your teacher's rough answers. Learn *where* the M/A marks live.
- **Every paper twice, three months apart.** The second pass tests memory vs. understanding.
- **Wrong-answer autopsy:** for each lost mark, one line in a log — topic, why, and the correct method. Re-read the log weekly.

## Exam-day technique (the A* differentiators)

1. **Read the command word before the question.** "Show that", "Hence", "Explain why" each demand different behaviour.
2. **Calculator discipline:** write the unrounded value in your working, round only at the end (questions specify 2 d.p. / 3 s.f. — obey exactly, or lose the A mark).
3. **Units and accuracy:** every mensuration answer needs units; every "estimate" answer needs 1 s.f. first.
4. **The last question** is designed to be unfamiliar. It's usually 2–3 chained parts, each using the previous. Even 3 of 8 marks there separates A from A\*.
5. **Never leave blanks.** An M-mark attempt with a wrong final answer still scores.

## FAQ

**Is 0580 the same as GCSE Maths?** Content-wise very close to the higher-tier English GCSE, but internationally administered, with slightly different weightings (more trigonometry and transformations than Edexcel/AQA higher tier).

**Core or Extended?** If you need a 7/A or above — Extended, always. Core caps at C.

**Are formulae given?** The formula sheet covers mensuration. Trig values, index laws, and circle theorems are NOT given — memorise them.

**How many past papers should I do?** Quality over quantity: ~15–20 full papers *marked properly with a mistake log* beats 40 papers done passively.

**What's the difference between 0580 and 0606?** 0606 is Additional Maths — a separate, harder qualification closer to AS level. 0580 is the standard IGCSE.

**Can I get an A\* with a weak Paper 2?** Possibly — papers combine to one grade. But Paper 4 carries 100 marks, so Paper 4 strength matters more.
